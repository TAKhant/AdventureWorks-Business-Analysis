Key Findings
Executive Summary
AdventureWorks generates strong revenue, but the analysis shows that the central business issue is not sales volume alone. The key challenge is how effectively revenue is converted into gross profit.
Overall, the business generated approximately £109.81M in sales, £12.55M in gross profit, and an overall gross margin of 11.43%. Performance varies significantly across sales channels, products, customers, and territories.
The analysis identified six major findings.

1. Strong Revenue, but Relatively Thin Overall Profitability
AdventureWorks generated approximately:
- Total Sales: £109.81M
- Gross Profit: £12.55M
- Gross Margin: 11.43%
The company operates at a large revenue scale, but only a relatively small proportion of sales is converted into gross profit.
This means that revenue alone is not sufficient to evaluate business performance. Gross profit and gross margin must also be monitored to understand the quality of that revenue.

2. Revenue Growth Does Not Always Translate Into Profitable Growth
The time analysis showed that sales growth was not always accompanied by gross profit growth.
For example, comparing July–December 2017 with July–December 2018:
- Sales increased by approximately 55.8%
- Gross profit decreased by approximately 6.5%
This shows that the company can generate higher revenue while becoming less profitable.
Management should therefore evaluate growth using both revenue and profitability measures rather than relying only on sales growth.

3. The Reseller Channel Is the Largest Profitability Problem
The Reseller channel generates the majority of company revenue but contributes very little gross profit.
Reseller
- Sales: approximately £80.45M
- Share of company sales: approximately 73%
- Gross Profit: approximately £0.47M
- Share of company gross profit: approximately 3.8%
- Gross Margin: approximately 0.58%
Internet
- Sales: approximately £29.36M
- Gross Profit: approximately £12.08M
- Gross Margin: approximately 41%
- Contribution to company gross profit: approximately 96%
In addition, more than half of active resellers are loss-making.
The analysis therefore shows that high Reseller revenue does not translate into strong profitability. The Reseller channel is the most important area for management investigation.

4. Product Performance Varies Significantly Despite Strong Overall Sales
Bikes dominate AdventureWorks revenue, accounting for approximately 86% of total company sales.
However, profitability differs significantly across products and subcategories.
Examples include:
- Mountain Bikes generate strong sales and strong gross profit.
- Touring Bikes generate substantial revenue but very weak margins.
- Jerseys generate negative gross profit.
- Caps generate high unit volume but negative gross margin.
- Some Touring Frames also generate weak or negative profitability.
This demonstrates that high sales or high unit volume does not automatically mean that a product is valuable to the business.
Product performance should therefore be evaluated using sales, gross profit, and gross margin together.

5. Repeat and High-Value Internet Customers Contribute Disproportionately
Repeat customers represent approximately 37% of Internet customers, but generate around:
- 77% of Internet sales
- 77% of Internet gross profit
The highest-spending 25% of Internet customers generate approximately 76% of Internet revenue.
This shows that customer value is highly concentrated.
Customer retention and the development of high-value customer relationships are therefore important drivers of profitable growth in the Internet channel.

6. Geographic Performance Is Strongly Influenced by Sales-Channel Mix
North America generates the majority of AdventureWorks revenue, but its profitability is weaker than some smaller markets.
North America
- Approximately 72% of company sales
- Gross Margin: approximately 6.9%
Pacific
- Approximately 10% of company sales
- Gross Margin: approximately 33.6%
The analysis shows that this difference is strongly connected to sales-channel mix.
Markets with heavy Reseller activity tend to have weaker profitability, while markets with stronger Internet sales tend to generate healthier gross margins.
This means geographic performance should not be evaluated using revenue alone. Channel composition must also be considered.
Overall Business Conclusion
AdventureWorks has strong revenue, but the main business challenge is profitable growth rather than sales growth alone.
The analysis shows that:
- High revenue does not always produce high gross profit.
- The Reseller channel is the largest profitability weakness.
- The Internet channel generates significantly stronger margins.
- Some high-sales and high-volume products are weak or loss-making.
- Repeat and high-value Internet customers are disproportionately valuable.
- Geographic profitability is strongly affected by channel mix.
The strongest opportunity for AdventureWorks is therefore to improve the profitability of existing revenue while protecting and expanding the products, customers, channels, and markets that generate the strongest gross profit.

Final Recommendation
AdventureWorks should make profitable growth its central management priority. The strongest immediate opportunity is to improve the Reseller channel and loss-making products while protecting the highly profitable Internet channel, valuable repeat customers, and stronger product and market segments.
